# First-build ideas

For a new user who arrives without an idea. Offer two or three from the group that matches their choice, in plain words, and let them pick one, change one or bring their own: their own idea always wins. Each idea is small enough for a first fleet to finish and merge within the hour, needs nothing beyond GitHub and the agent they already use (no paid service, key or other sign-in), and shows the factory working end to end: a short grill, a few tickets, builders, a review and a merge. Whatever they pick still starts with its short grill, and every action gate still asks first: creating a repository, any write, spend, secrets and publishing.

## A small change in a repository they have

- **A clearer README**: a short "what this is and how to run it" section; it touches no code, and anyone can check it by reading it.
- **One missing test**: a test for a function that has none; it proves itself by passing, and the review shows how the fleet checks work.
- **A friendlier error message**: one confusing message rewritten to say what went wrong and what to do next; small, visible and easy to review.
- **A `--help` line**: a usage message for a script or command that lacks one; one file, and they can run it the moment it merges.
- **One small visible tweak**: a dark-mode switch, a version in the footer or a fixed typo on a page; they see their own project change.

## A fresh project from an idea

- **A to-do app**: add, tick off and delete tasks in the browser, saved on their own machine; everyone already knows what done looks like.
- **A sports-team dashboard**: one team's recent results and next game on a single page, from a small saved data file; personal, and it looks good fast.
- **A personal site**: one page with who they are, their links and a project or two; something they will actually want to share.
- **A reading or recipe list**: add an entry, mark it done and filter the list; a real little app with no server.
- **A unit or tip converter**: type a number and see the answer; tiny, useful, and easy to try in the review.

## Something they've always wanted

Build the first small slice of the wish, one they can use within the hour; the rest can come in later fleets.

- **A hobby log**: record runs, games played or plants watered, and see the list; the first page of the app they have meant to build.
- **A simple browser game**: tic-tac-toe or a memory card game; fun to play the moment it merges.
- **A countdown page**: the days until a trip, a launch or a birthday; one page with a clear finish line.
- **A quiz on something they love**: ten questions and a score at the end; easy to grill, and theirs from the first question.
- **A chore-saving script**: the first version of a tool they wish they had, such as renaming files by date in a folder they choose; it saves them time from day one.
